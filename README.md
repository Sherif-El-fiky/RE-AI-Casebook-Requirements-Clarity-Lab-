# Requirements Clarity Lab

**AI-assisted Requirements Engineering, with the judgment to know what to keep.**

I'm Sherif ElFiky, a Requirements Engineer / Technical Project Manager with 15+ years across automotive and defence programs (Bosch, Volkswagen, Stellantis, Porsche), IREB CPRE-certified. This repo is a small, honest showcase of how I use AI tools inside real RE work - not as a replacement for RE judgment, but as an amplifier for it.

Each case study follows the same structure:

1. **Problem statement** - a realistic RE scenario, based on the most common documented RE failure modes (ambiguous, underspecified, and incomplete requirements are consistently ranked as the top industry-wide RE problems in academic surveys such as NaPiRE).
2. **Raw input** - messy, real-world-style stakeholder input, the kind every RE actually receives.
3. **AI-assisted analysis** - the prompts I used and what the AI produced.
4. **Refined requirements** - the final, testable requirement set.
5. **Reflection** - where the AI output was useful, where it was wrong, generic, or missing context, and what I changed and why. This is the part that actually matters: an RE's value isn't running the AI, it's knowing which of its suggestions to trust.

All scenarios are synthetic and domain-neutral. No client, employer, or project-confidential material appears anywhere in this repository - every example here is built from public, generic requirements-engineering scenarios I constructed myself.

## Why this exists

Requirements Engineering is entering a phase where AI can speed up first drafts, flag ambiguity, and suggest structure. What it can't do is own the trade-offs, catch a subtly wrong assumption, or know when a "reasonable-sounding" AI suggestion doesn't actually fit the system's real constraints. That judgment is still the job. This repo is my record of practicing that combination deliberately, not just talking about it.
